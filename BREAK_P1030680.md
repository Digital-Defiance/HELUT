### 4 — The partial-stecker discovery

Apparent “bombe→Enigma translation bug”: survivors failed `hasPrefix(crib)`. Isolated debug (`--debug-survivor`) proved otherwise:

- Stepping matches the bombe trail 18/18
- Known-good key through the same initializer decrypts P1030684 perfectly
- All mismatches land on letters the bombe never pinned

**Cause:** one-letter seed → one connected component. Split menus return half a board. Forensics (`--bombe-inspect`): `UUUVIRSIBENNULEINS@0` has 3 components; `{BSTVZ}` admits **0/26** seeds — the setting is logically dead. All 12 stops had **0** joint ≤10-plug completions (`logs/discriminator-run.log`). Stage 2 (stecker completion across every component) became mandatory.

Not a new crib wedge — a greenfield datapath that melts Yosys LUT6 netlists into

#### Challenges and adaptations

**1. Dependency-chain shatter (full wipe).** Explore climbed \(F_{crypto}\) **−4.59 → −1.22**
(non-binary 58,276 → 30,857). Squeeze λ→~10 snapped weights toward binary (non-binary →
**14,010**) but **regressed crypto to −8.04**. Continuous soft paths that ignore rotor
wiring look good under λ=0 and die under discreteness. Artifact
`enigma_m4_tensorlut_coldstart.v` is historical junk, not rediscovered Enigma.

**2. Targeted melt + freeze mask.** Adaptation: freeze known-good LUT blocks; wipe only the
unknown region. Tiny nets (2-bit adder) rediscover under melt masks — the loop works when
the genotype is the right size.

**3. Cone tagging without hierarchy.** Abc-flattened `enigma_m4` has no `plugboard` cell
names. Naive reverse BFS from CT/PT floods ~700 LUTs (state-polluted cloud). Adaptation:
`TensorLUTConeTagger` — CT-side closure that refuses state-Q wires; PT is registered so
unwrap Q→D then reverse *outside* the polluted cloud. Result: forward
`105–111,113,115,117,119` + reverse `397,399,542,786,902` = **16** melt indices.

**4. INIT melt ≠ stecker.** Melting those 16 still shattered under λ (explore −1.90; squeeze
crypto −4.94 with 118 melt fractions). Early resignation (`resignNonBinaryAbove` at
λ≥½λmax) correctly aborts doomed lineages — baseline identity-stecker control: 3 RESIGNs
at λ≈5 with hundreds of fractional melt weights (`logs/tensorlut-m4-stecker-resign-control.log`).
**Root cause:** Verilog `plugboard()` is identity `clamp26`. The cone LUTs are I/O codecs,
not a 26×26 involution. Independent INIT floats + isolated \(w(1-w)\) never enforce
A↔X reciprocity.

**5. Stecker involution by construction.** Adaptation: freeze the entire known-good TensorLUT
core (identity stecker in silicon). Chromosome = `SteckerInvolution` (≤10 disjoint pairs).
Sandwich: inject `S(CT)` → frozen core → score soft PT vs bits of `S(P)`. The GA cannot
invent non-reciprocal maps. Identity seed on `TEST`/`YCPY` @ AAA → \(F_{crypto}=0\).

**6. Blind rediscovery + sequential plugs.** Short cribs admit false \(F=0\) steckers and leave
unused plugs unconstrained. Adaptation: grade **active-map** match on CT∪PT; **parsimony**;

#### 49.2 — Middle-ring arm: 6.5–11.4×, not 26×

The middle ring was pinned by a literal `0` in one host tuple, not by any kernel
assumption. The reason it could not simply be swept is cost, and the reason it is cheaper
than 26× is an equivalence:

> The trail depends only on *window* positions — notch tests are ring-free. So if neither a
> lane's middle wheel nor that of its ring-A partner (window `m − ρ`) reaches its own notch
> across the span, the middle wheel steps only on right-wheel notches — the same times in
> both trails — the left wheel never moves, and `offsetM = m − ρ` *is* the partner's window.
> Same scramblers, same verdict. A middle-notch hit breaks the invariant, and those are
> exactly the lanes a middle-ring sweep must actually test.

So ring A runs in full and rings B–Z run only their notch-hitting lanes. The union is
complete coverage, not a sample.

Graded two ways, because a coverage claim that drops keys is the same bug as 49.1:

|---|---|
| Host predicate, 27-letter menu, IV-III-VIII (1-notch middle) | 1,368,224 / 1,757,600 lanes covered (77.8%), **0 verdict mismatches**, cost **6.5×** |
| Host predicate, same, I-VI-VII (2-notch middle) | 1,027,520 / 1,757,600 (58.5%), **0 mismatches**, cost **11.4×** |
| Through the kernel, rings B/F/N, both triples | 74–90% of lanes skipped, **~5,600–6,900 survivors actually discarded per ring**, and every discarded verdict carried identically by its ring-A partner. Violations: covered-but-kept **0**, skipped-but-not-covered **0**, partner-disagrees **0** |

#### 49.3 — Throughput: in-kernel plug sieve + GPU seed mask

The kernel applied **no** plug budget; `maxPlugs` lived only on the host, so every
over-budget stop was shipped back and re-derived before being thrown away. And the driver
read only `survivors[lane] != 0`, discarding the 26-bit seed mask the GPU had already
computed, then re-ran all 26 host closures per surviving lane.

### 50 — Crib-free path: the Ostwald/Weierud attack, measured

A second engine, on a different principle. Every arm in Phases 0–23 is crib-driven: the
Welchman board needs a probable word, and Phase 13 says every crib we own is imported
register from other nets. Ostwald and Weierud's ciphertext-only attack needs **no crib** —
for each candidate rotor setting it hill-climbs the plugboard and ranks the setting by the
score the climb reaches. Published reach: messages down to about 100 letters, shortest ever
broken **78**, on *three-rotor Heer* traffic. P1030680 is **72** letters on four-rotor naval
M4 — shorter than the record, on a harder machine.

**The wall is not compute, and that matters for how to spend hardware.** 72 letters of naval
German carries ~223 bits of redundancy; a ten-plug board is ~47 bits of nuisance parameter
*fitted per candidate*; the shell × position space is ~10¹¹. Taking the maximum of an
overfitted score over that many candidates is a multiple-comparisons problem, and adding
candidates makes it **worse** by raising the maximum of the noise. Enigma@Home has vastly
more aggregate compute than one Mac and stops at 78. So the decisive quantity is not a rate:

> **margin = climbed score at the true setting − best climbed score over wrong settings**

Positive, and a sweep can find the key. Negative, and the search ranks noise above signal
no matter the hardware.

#### 50.1 — Harness, and a preflight that caught the corpus

`--ostwald-curve` measures that margin at a ladder of truncation lengths over the published
1 May 1945 U-534 keys — same net, same signals office, same German dialect as the target.
Wrong settings are drawn from the *same shell*, differing only in message key: the hardest
available negative control, since a real sweep also enumerates wrong wheel orders and rings,
which are easier to reject. Exhaustion is applied symmetrically to true and wrong settings,
because giving the truth 141 attempts to maximise while each decoy gets one would manufacture
a margin out of nothing.

The harness refuses to print a curve unless its controls decrypt their own published
plaintext under their own published key. That preflight immediately found something:
**32 of 48 controls round-trip exactly**, and the rest do not.

**Correction, same day (Phase 50.9).** That was first written up here as "transcription garble
in the archive, independent corroboration of Phase 22." **That reading was overstated** and is
withdrawn. Classifying the 17 discrepant controls by error shape shows at least three
separable causes, and only the last is garble:

* **Multi-part messages with per-part keys.** `P1030675` (719 letters), `P1030688` (472) and
  others exceed the ~320-letter Kriegsmarine limit, so they were transmitted in parts with a
  fresh Grundstellung each. Decrypting end-to-end under one message key *must* produce a
  correct head and a garbage tail — which is exactly the observed shape (errors from position
  298 of 719, from 143 of 208, and so on). Not an archive defect at all; a harness assumption.
* **Indels / transcript divergence.** `P1030711` (98 letters, errors from position 22) is far
  too short for the multi-part explanation, so it is a genuine shift or a divergent transcript.
  Girard documents exactly this in the sister message: an entire four-letter group `HMHY`
  present on the Schlüsselzettel and blank on the plain-paper copy.
* **Isolated substitutions — the real garble signal, and it is thin.** Only **4** controls show
  scattered single-letter errors (`P1030708` 3, `P1030695` 2, `P1030706` 2, `P1030707` 1):
  **8 events in total.**

Eight events cannot support a confusion model, so the prior for any degarbler has to come from
Girard's direct reading of the originals — he names handwriting confusions **U↔N, Q↔G, H↔F**
"etc." — rather than from this corpus. Recorded because the first version of this paragraph
claimed more than the data does. `Scripts/garble_model.py`, `logs/garble-model-2026-08-17.log`.

#### 50.2 — Validation: the implementation is correct

At 372 letters the staged climb recovers **10 of 10 plugs and 100% of the plaintext**, margin

#### 50.6 — The bombe coupling: at 72 letters, 4 plugs flips the sign

The lever only this repository can pull, measured before building the wiring. `enigma-cuda`
documents `-s <plugs>` as "known plugboard connections, e.g. from running a bombe" — and then
requires them typed in by hand, because the bombe is someone else's program. Here both engines
share a binary, and the quarantine tier already stores soft-band Welchman stops *with the
stecker each menu forced* (15–24 of 26 letters, i.e. 7–12 plugs).

Modelled fairly: the true setting is seeded with *k* genuinely correct plugs (what a true stop
forces) while every decoy is seeded with *k* **random** plugs (what a ghost stop forces).
Seeded plugs are immovable, which is the point — they leave the fit. At **72 letters**, the
target's exact length, identical 10 controls:

| Seeded plugs | win rate | median margin | plugs recovered | letters right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 40% | −0.1503 | 0/10 | 8% |
| 2 | 20% | −0.0991 | 2/10 | 10% |
| **4** | 70% | **+0.1296** | 6/10 | 44% |
| **6** | 90% | **+0.6656** | 8/10 | 85% |
| **8** | **100%** | **+1.0488** | **10/10** | **100%** |

**The margin crosses zero at four seeded plugs; at eight the attack recovers the complete key
and 100% of the plaintext at 72 letters, on every control.** That is below the published
78-letter record, on the harder four-rotor machine.

Read it precisely, because it is easy to over-read. This is **oracle-seeded**: the correct
plugs come from the known key, so it assumes the stop being seeded from *is* the true key. It
does **not** make the attack crib-free at 72 letters. What it establishes is narrower and
still valuable: **a true bombe stop is finishable at 72 letters.** The climber can take a stop
the strict IC/tail bar rejected — because of garble, or short-message noise — and recover the
rest of the board and the plaintext from it.

#### 50.8 — Naval-dialect model: a reasoned guess that measured null

Ostwald names the measure as the hard part, and the generic model is 28.5M letters of the
*wrong* German: Kriegsmarine traffic is a dialect with `X`/`Y` as punctuation, spelled digits

64 GB of unified memory invites higher order — 5-grams are 11.9M cells, 6-grams 309M, both
resident — but you cannot *estimate* a 6-gram model from the 7,537 letters of naval plaintext
that exist. So the play was domain match at low order: count trigrams on the 48 decrypts and
mix them into the generic model as a Dirichlet prior, **leave-one-out**, since the controls
under attack are themselves in that corpus and fitting on them would score the model's memory
of the answer.

| Letters | generic margin | naval margin | generic z | naval z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 72 | −0.2735 | −0.2763 | 0.12 | −0.05 |
| 100 | −0.0996 | −0.1048 | 1.01 | 1.08 |
| 140 | −0.1062 | −0.1168 | 0.52 | 0.91 |
| 180 | −0.0114 | −0.0277 | 1.49 | 1.25 |
| 252 | +0.4957 | **+0.5428** | 8.14 | **10.78** |

**Null at every length that matters.** Margins sit within run-to-run noise of each other and
win rates are identical or marginally worse. The one real effect is at 252 letters, where

nothing at the crossing point. 7,537 letters is too little evidence to move a 17,576-cell table.

Caveat that keeps this an honest negative rather than a closed one: the mixing mass κ was set
to 1200 and **never tuned**. A κ sweep is the obvious follow-up, and smaller κ (more trust in
the naval counts) is exactly the direction that risks overfitting on 7.5k letters.

### 51 — The Mulein board: a diagonal board that counts contradictions

Engine work, and the first mechanism in this campaign that the 1945 hardware could not have
implemented **in principle** rather than merely slowly. No key, no plaintext, no crib
hypothesis changed. As of this entry it has produced **no P1030680 verdict**.

**Why relays cannot do this.** On the physical bombe a contradiction *is* current finding a
second path through the diagonal board: the hypothesis short-circuits and the drum spins on.
Copper cannot answer "how many contradictions?" — the relay either lights or it does not, and
every faithful reimplementation inherits that, `WelchmanBombe.propagate` included, which
returns nil on the first doubled row. That is fatal in the presence of garble: a single
mis-transcribed ciphertext letter contradicts a *true* menu and the board discards the real
key without comment. Every exact-crib clean negative in this campaign is therefore a negative
about the **recorded** ciphertext, not necessarily the transmitted one.

In silicon we can count instead of short-circuit, and ask the question a relay cannot phrase:

> **How few menu edges must be deleted before this setting closes consistently?**

Deleting ≤ *t* edges and accepting the setting is the **deletion-tolerant diagonal board** —
locally, the **Mulein board**. It occupies the same structural slot Welchman's diagonal board
did: not a new bombe, a new board on it. Tolerance 0 is the historical board bit for bit.

#### 51.1 — The first implementation was a total no-op, for two compounding reasons

Tolerance must be implemented by **removing** edges before propagating, not by abandoning
them mid-flight. The first attempt did the latter and measured as a complete no-op — one
survivor at every tolerance level. Both causes are worth keeping:

1. **The bad value is already committed.** A garbled edge processed early writes a wrong σ(x)
   into `live`, which then propagates through other edges *and the diagonal board*. Restoring
   the two rows that just conflicted unwinds none of that.
2. **The conflict surfaces at the wrong edge.** Whichever edge happens to be visited when the
   inconsistency becomes visible gets blamed, and that is typically a *correct* edge rather
   than the garbled one, so dropping it does not help and the real culprit stays in.

Enumerating the dropped set is order-independent and reuses the validated exact closure rather
than a second hand-rolled one. `propagateCore` is now the single implementation of the board's
logic, shared by the exact and tolerant entry points.

#### 51.2 — Sensitivity: the exact board really does throw the key away

Known key, P1030684, 27-letter crib at offset 0, corrupting letters inside the crib span
(avoiding self-encipherment, which would make the placement illegal rather than merely
garbled):

| Garbled letters | tolerance 0 | tolerance = g | plugs forced (correct/total) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | KEPT | KEPT | 25/25 |
| 1 | **LOST** | **KEPT** | **25/25** |
| 2 | **LOST** | **KEPT** | **25/25** |
| 3 | **LOST** | **KEPT** | **25/25** |

That LOST → KEPT transition is what makes the mechanism real rather than decorative, and it is

only **4** correct plugs to flip the crib-free margin positive at 72 letters. The two halves

Tolerance *widens* what survives, so the price is survivor inflation. Measured on the clean
ciphertext over all 26⁴ lanes at the true shell. At offset 0 the result looked like a crisp

| 17–19 | 1 | 1 | ~134,000 |

global switch. The campaign's `--bombe-min-crib 16` cannot simply be run at tolerance 1 — at
offset 0 that is 130,787 survivors per shell against an escalator that has handled 86.

#### 51.7 — Spliced menus: the indel hypothesis, graded

Girard's actual finding was not a substitution but a **missing four-letter group** (`HMHY`,
present on the Schlüsselzettel copy and blank on the plain-paper one). That is a different
failure mode from anything the Mulein board models, and it needed saying out loud: **the indel
board is not part of the Mulein board.** They are siblings covering the two halves of edit
distance.

| Error | What went wrong | Fix | Board |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substitution (`U`→`N`) | Wrong *letter*, known position | Delete a menu edge | Mulein board, tolerance ≥ 1 |
| Indel (group dropped) | *Correct* letters, *shifted* positions | Re-index edges, shift step numbers | **Exact board**, new menu geometry |

**The model.** With `R` the recorded ciphertext and `T` the transmitted one, `δ` letters missing
at `T`-position `p` gives `R[j] = T[j]` for `j < p` and `R[j] = T[j+δ]` after. For a crib at
`T`-offset `q`, edge `i` takes rotor step `q+i` (a `T` coordinate — the rotor does not care what
the transcriber wrote) but ciphertext letter `R[q+i]` before the gap and `R[q+i-δ]` after, with
the `δ` letters inside the gap **lost** and their edges dropped. Step number and ciphertext
index *diverge*, which is exactly why this cannot be expressed as an ordinary menu, where
`step == cipherIndex` by construction.

**It needed no kernel change.** `BombeMenu.steps` was already a separate array from
`BombeMenu.ends`, and the Metal kernel already selects a scrambler per edge from `edgeStep[]`.
So a spliced menu runs on Welchman's **exact** board — nothing relaxed, and therefore **no
survivor inflation and no pre-qualification gate**, unlike tolerance.

**Sensitivity (graded, `logs/indel-selftest-2026-08-17.log`).** Deleted a real four-letter group
(`HBSX`) from the P1030684 ciphertext at position 12 to manufacture the transcript a copyist
would have left, then attacked the damaged text with a 27-letter crib straddling the gap:

| Menu | Verdict | Plugs forced |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary menu, recorded offset (what every prior arm runs) | **ILLEGAL** — self-encipherment | — |
| Ordinary menu on the undamaged text (upper bound) | KEPT | 25/25 |
| **Spliced menu, true splice + delta** | **KEPT** | **25/25** |

The naive menu does not merely lose the key — the misalignment makes the placement illegal, so
the true shell was never even testable. The spliced menu recovers it with all 25 plug deductions
correct, on 23 edges (four fell in the gap).

**Specificity is UNMEASURED on the board, and that is the finding.** At the 16-letter doctrine
floor, **all 28 wrong splices were eliminated by self-encipherment before the bombe was asked**
(0 reached the board). Two honest readings follow:

* *Favourable, structurally.* Enigma never encodes a letter to itself, so a menu built from the
  true crib at the true offset with the true splice is **always** legal — it reconstructs real
  plain/cipher pairs. **Legality can never reject the truth**, while it killed 28 of 28 wrong
  candidates for free with no GPU at all. The splice search is largely self-pruning.
* *But the board is not doing the work.* The discrimination comes from the cipher's own
  constraint, and legality prunes by coincidence rather than correctness. A spliced hit therefore
  needs independent confirmation, exactly as a short-crib stop does.

**Two self-inflicted test bugs, recorded because both produced confident wrong answers.** The
first probe kept the sensitivity crib's start while shortening its length, which walked the crib
off the gap entirely — and since the damaged and undamaged transcripts are *identical before the
gap*, every splice produced the same correct menu and the tool reported "28/28 wrong splices
surviving." The second auto-shortened to 12 letters, below the campaign's own ghost threshold
(menu 627 was 14 letters and produced 193M raw stops), where nothing discriminates. The probe now
forces the crib to straddle the gap and refuses to go below 16.

**Enumeration cost.** One 27-letter crib generates **302** distinct spliced menus (269 straddling,
33 post-gap) against ~25 ordinary placements — roughly 12×. The post-gap family is the strongest
(full edge count, loops 9) and is the cheap first bite: it is the ordinary catalog with every step
number `δ` higher, i.e. "a group went missing somewhere before this crib." Crib-entirely-before-
the-gap cases are byte-identical to ordinary menus and are skipped rather than re-swept.

#### 51.8 — Both mechanisms in one sweep, graded together

Tolerance was a *global* config flag, which contradicts 51.4's own finding that inflation is a
property of the individual menu. It is now **per-menu**: `toleranceFor(menu:)` gates on loop
count and the sieve is set inside the per-menu loop. That is safe with the shell pipeline
because `enqueue` copies the tolerance into the command buffer at encode time, so shells still
in flight from the previous menu keep their own value.

That makes a mixed run possible, which is the right way to run both boards at once.
`--bombe-indel <delta>` folds the spliced family into the same menu list, so one saturated
command queue carries **spliced menus at tolerance 0** — they need none, a splice relaxes
nothing — alongside **loop-rich menus at tolerance 1**. Two separate processes would merely
timeshare the one GPU queue and each take about twice as long for the same finish time.

Graded on the blind control with **both** mechanisms live: 910 spliced menus added (delta 4,
group-aligned splices) plus tolerance 1, rings pinned `AAAH`
(`logs/control-indel-tolerance-2026-08-17.log`):

| Menu | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `…MITU@45` — a **wrong** spliced hypothesis, loops 9, tolerance 1 applied | **dead at the board** |
| `…MITU@0` — the true ordinary menu | ***BREAK FOUND*** — UKW B / γ / IV-III-VIII / `AAAH` / `VYYN`, all ten plugs `BQ CH DI EJ GL KP NV OU SZ TY`, IC 0.064, tail −2.848 |

**1 raw stop over 1.228×10⁹ settings, and it was the truth.** So adding 910 spliced menus and
tolerance 1 inflated the control's stop count by nothing, did not fabricate a false break, and
did not lose the real one. Throughput fell to ~1.0M/s (from 45.4M/s) under tolerance 1 plus
contention with the running target arm, consistent with the 15.3× measured factor.

#### 51.9 — Pricing an indel arm against the target, and the post-gap restriction

Measured rather than estimated. `--bombe-indel 4` on the 24-placement strongest fixture (14
distinct crib texts) generates **1,684** spliced menus, taking the fixture to 1,708 placements.
Spliced menus run on the exact board at full rate, so:

| Arm | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| All 1,684 × right rings | ~5.9 min/menu → **~7 days** | too much for a first bite |
| All 1,684 × rings AAAA | ~6.4 h | **near-vacuous** — AAAA covers 0/26 at 40 letters |
| **Post-gap only × right rings** (`--bombe-indel-post-gap`) | 237 menus → **~23 h** | the arm to run |

`--bombe-indel-post-gap` keeps only the family whose crib lies entirely *after* the gap,
dropping 1,447 straddling menus. The restriction is principled, not arbitrary:

* It is the cleanest form of the hypothesis — *a group went missing somewhere before this crib,
  so every step number is δ too low* — and it is one menu per legal offset, independent of where
  before the crib the gap actually sat.
* It carries the **full edge count**. Nothing fell in the gap, so no deduction power is lost,
  whereas a straddling menu forfeits up to δ edges precisely when it can least afford to.
* It is ~11–14% of the family, and the straddling remainder is both weaker and roughly nine
  times more numerous — the worst ratio of cost to deduction power in the whole construction.

#### 51.10 — The indel family is **not** a ghost factory (graded), and the first target results

The affordability of an indel arm turns entirely on whether 237 extra menus manufacture false
positives. Tolerance had to be pre-qualified per menu for exactly that reason. Splicing does not,
and now that is measured rather than argued.
