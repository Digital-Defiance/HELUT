\chapter*{How to read the living boxes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to read the living boxes}

Five boxes appear throughout this book.
They are not decoration; they are the difference between a textbook and a press release.

\reproduced{C\textit{n}}{A result that someone else can re-run from \corpus{REPRODUCE.md} and the claim inventory. Lecture voice may treat it as established \emph{in this corpus}.}

\hedgebox{H\textit{n}}{An honest asterisk. Say it in class with the hedge attached. Do not let students drop the asterisk in a problem set or a paper.}

\frontierbox{Speculative territory from \corpus{directives/potential-avenues.md}. Assign as a seminar essay, never as a midterm fact.}

\growswhen{A stub that is waiting on a new \cid{} row, a closed \hid{}, or a graduated avenue. Skip, or treat as a reading of the matching directive.}

\scaffoldbox{This PDF is not a finished textbook and is not ready to teach.
Edition~\corpusedition{} was bootstrapped with AI as a long-term scaffold:
a place to collect theorems, formulae, and claim-sheet receipts as the
laboratory moves. Humans must vet, bulk up, fill in, and edit every chapter
before it is a course. Do not assign it as a university class.
Claims, lemmas, theorems, and formulae in this volume must not be invented
by the model: a human checks each against \corpus{directives/claim-sheet.md}
and a reproduce receipt \emph{before commit}.}
