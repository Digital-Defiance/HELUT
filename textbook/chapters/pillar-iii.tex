\section{Why a cryptanalysis lab must design ciphers}

A course that only breaks historical machines trains curators.
Pillar~III asks Blue to sit in the same room as Red: mutate the non-linear
feedback under adversarial pressure, and fail closed when the invariants
break~\cite{helut-enigma256,helut-release}.

The historical Enigma leaks this laboratory proved fatal---26-letter menus,
self-stecker ban, thin plugboards, odometer stepping, paper day keys---are
deleted on purpose in Enigma256 (E256).
The reciprocal rotor \emph{contract} is kept: encrypt $\equiv$ decrypt under
the same machine state.

\section{The E256 contract, for lecture}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Alphabet is a byte, not 26 letters.
  \item Plugboard is a 256-entry involution, 128 pairs, no fixed points.
  \item Four active rotors drawn from a 16-rotor day pool; un-reflector
        allows fixed points.
  \item After each byte, NLFF step enables advance offsets; a Galois LFSR
        clocks. Not an odometer.
  \item Control-plane crypto (KEM, HKDF, AEAD, signatures) never enters
        datapath BRAMs.
  \item The live field is Apple Silicon SoftBus, not an FPGA board on the
        critical path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theorem 2 (structural SoftBus)}
\label{sec:thm-enigma256}

\begin{theorem}[Enigma256 SoftBus reciprocity / fail-closed; \corpusthm{2}]
\label{thm:enigma256}
Under the hypotheses of \texttt{Enigma256Formal.certificate()}
(reciprocal scramble path; day-key involution builders; NLFF retaps do not
rewrite frozen scramble): frozen scramble is a permutation and an involution;
stream encrypt-then-decrypt recovers plaintext under identical keys; derived
plugboard is fixed-point-free involution and un-reflector is an involution;
\texttt{hardenedCubic()} rejects \texttt{coupledCubic6}.
\end{theorem}

Machine-checked: \texttt{Enigma256Formal.certificate()} /
\texttt{testEnigma256FormalCertificate}.

\begin{warning}
Theorem~2 is a \emph{structural SoftBus contract}---not IND-CPA, not a claim
that Red TensorLUT/KPA/\texttt{ent} cannot force Blue generation rolls, and
not a finished polymorphic-cipher standard.
\end{warning}

\section{Red pressure}

Red is not a separate team with a PDF of attacks.
Red is TensorLUT cones, SoftBus known-plaintext attacks, and
\texttt{ent} on the keystream---on the same Mac that rolls Blue
generations.

A generation that cannot hold under that pressure does not ship.
Fail-closed means: if reciprocity, bijection, or a stated invariant
breaks, the roll aborts rather than emitting a ``mostly fine'' Verilog.
Theorem~2 covers the structural abort path for coupled NLFF harden;
empirical Red batteries remain separate grades.

\section{What students design}

\begin{exercise}[Blue studio]
Propose one additional invariant E256 should fail-closed on (example:
NLFF period lower bound; stecker-free involution; BRAM burst width).
Write the test that would refuse a generation. Do not implement a new
cipher in this edition unless the instructor opens a project slot.
\end{exercise}

\end{exercise}

\section{Relation to Pillars I and II}

E256's lecture arc is the structural SoftBus contract (Theorem~\ref{thm:enigma256}).
\cid{39} is a frozen 1-round scramble under GGSW Metal SING---not live BRAM,
not NLFF, not a messaging product.
Calling that lab ``production encrypted E256'' is a \claimkind{N}.

\nonclaimbox{E256 is not a NIST candidate in this book, not a replacement
for TLS, and not an implication that polymorphic rotors beat lattices.
It is a pedagogical and laboratory standard for fail-closed mutation.}
